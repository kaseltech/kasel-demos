General Exam Tips

  1. Describe your worst days, not your best. The examiner needs to understand the full impact of each condition on your life.
  2. Don't push through pain during ROM testing. Stop at the point where pain begins. If they ask you to keep going, tell them it hurts.
  3. Quantify everything. "3-4 times per week lasting 4-6 hours" is better than "it happens a lot." Put numbers on frequency, duration, and severity.
  4. Mention flare-ups and estimate functional loss. The examiner is required to document this. If you're not in a flare during the exam, tell them what it's like when you are.
  5. Connect the dots to service. Anything that started during your 2006-2007 or 2009-2010 Iraq deployments — carrying gear, rucking, standing on hard surfaces, stress, environmental exposures.
  6. Mention secondary connections. ED from MDD medications. Headaches from MDD/stress. Right ankle from pes planus/altered gait. Left knee from altered gait.
  7. If the examiner doesn't ask about something, volunteer it. Especially the nerve symptoms in your right foot. If it's not in the exam notes, it didn't happen as far as the VA is concerned.
  8. Don't minimize. Veterans often understate their conditions out of habit. Be honest about how these conditions actually affect your daily life, work, and activities.
  9. This is not a treatment appointment. The examiner is evaluating, not treating. Their job is to document the severity of your conditions. Your job is to make sure nothing gets left out.
